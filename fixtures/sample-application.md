# Community Green Innovation Fund — Draft Application

## Applicant

**Organization:** Green Neighbors Collective  
**Organization type:** Community environmental non-profit  
**Service area:** North Creek and nearby neighborhoods

Green Neighbors Collective is a community organization operating since 2022. We organize local cleanups and help residents learn about composting and water conservation. We are applying for support to establish a neighborhood composting and native-plant demonstration project.

## Project description

The project will place shared compost bins at two community gardens and create native-plant demonstration beds at a local park. Volunteers will host seasonal workshops on composting and water-wise gardening. Our coordinator will arrange sites and supplies, and volunteers will help maintain the gardens. We expect setup to begin in the spring and community activities to continue during the year.

The project will reduce household organic waste and improve habitat for pollinators. We plan to invite residents from North Creek to workshops and share information through community groups.

During the first part of the project, our coordinator will contact garden managers, identify accessible locations for bins, and speak with residents about the kinds of materials they already compost. We would like to make participation easy for renters and households without outdoor space. A volunteer group will help with installation and routine checks, while workshop hosts will demonstrate how to keep the bins usable through different seasons.

We have organized neighborhood cleanups, shared environmental information at local events, and worked with volunteers who care about the creek and nearby green spaces. These activities have helped us build relationships with residents, although we have not included attendance lists or outcome records with this application. We expect community interest to grow as the compost sites become visible and useful.

The work will be coordinated by our part-time project coordinator with assistance from volunteers. We will ask garden contacts to help schedule access and will use community noticeboards and local online groups to announce activities. We expect the work to take place over the coming year, with planning and site preparation first, installation after locations are agreed, and workshops continuing afterward. We have not set out a detailed month-by-month schedule or assigned named individuals to every activity.

We hope to make the compost available to gardeners and use the resulting material in the demonstration beds. The native plants will show residents examples that are suited to local conditions. We expect these visible activities to encourage more people to take part in environmental projects across the neighborhood.

## Continuation and outcomes

We hope the project will keep growing and become a lasting resource for the neighborhood. The gardens should remain useful after the grant, and we will look for volunteers and other community support to help continue the work.

We will encourage participants to keep using the compost sites and will invite local gardeners to share responsibility for routine care. If residents find the workshops helpful, we can consider offering more sessions in the future. We are optimistic that the community will continue to support the work, although we have not identified committed post-grant funding, a maintenance agreement, or a specific continuation partner.
